Biography

I am a Ph.D. student from the Electronic Engineering and Multimedia Lab (MMLab) of The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK), supervised by Prof. Xiaogang Wang and Prof. Hongsheng Li. My research primarily focuses on two fundamental challenges in the field of large models (e.g., Large Language Models): Reasoning and Efficiency. Specifically, My contributions span both areas:

Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design for Efficient AI System:

Scaling laws in AI have driven rapid model growth, creating challenges in training, deployment, and energy efficiency. I addressed these through parameter sparsification and low-precision techniques, inventing the power-of-two format [ICLR 2017] and the general N:M sparsity pattern [ICLR 2021], alongside efficient training methods like SR-STE [ICLR 2021]. These innovations are widely applied in large language and diffusion models, with successful hardware integration (e.g., Intel Arria, Movidius). Notably, my N:M sparsity model deployment on FPGA, published in IEEE VLSI, won the 2025 Best Paper Award, the first-ever for China, including HK and Macau.

Reasoning in (Multimodal) Large Language Models:

Reasoning, whether for humans or machines, is a hallmark of intelligence. Starting from the domain of mathematical reasoning, I proposed a reasoning paradigm based on interleaved text and code [ICLR 2024], which, compared to Chain-of-Thought (CoT), significantly elevated mathematical reasoning to the level of competition participants for the first time. This paradigm has become a standard in both open-source communities and proprietary models. Additionally, I developed a multimodal reasoning benchmark, MathVision [NeurIPS 2024], which has become the standard for evaluating the reasoning capabilities of nearly all mainstream multimodal AI models (e.g., Qwen-VL, Intern-VL, Seed-VL, etc.).
